## A pause in the bond selloff gives equities room to run

US equities reached fresh records on October 6 as Treasury yields retreated from multi-year highs and crude prices stabilised. The S&P 500 gained 0.6%, the Nasdaq 0.45% and the Dow 0.5%. Brent traded around $100.6 a barrel and WTI near $89.44.

The move is better read as relief than a reversal in financial conditions. Long-duration technology stocks benefited as the rise in discount rates paused, while expectations for AI-linked earnings remained strong. But sovereign supply, energy-driven inflation and elevated long yields have not gone away. Third-quarter earnings now matter because they will test whether heavy AI spending is producing operating income quickly enough to justify valuations.

## Oil supply is recovering faster than inventories and refined fuels

The US Energy Information Administration sharply raised its outlook on October 6. It now expects Brent to average $105 a barrel in the fourth quarter of 2026, $14 above its previous forecast, after an average of $114 in September. EIA estimates global petroleum inventories fell by 1.9 million barrels a day in the third quarter and will decline by another 0.7 million b/d in the fourth.

The bottleneck is increasingly downstream. Alternative pipelines, overland routes and ship-to-ship transfers should gradually restore Middle Eastern crude flows, but US East Coast distillate inventories were still 32% below their five-year seasonal average in September. EIA expects them to remain 20%-30% below average through winter. US diesel averaged $6.29 a gallon in September and is expected to remain above $6 in October.

That leaves diesel, heating oil and aviation exposed even if crude transit improves. EIA expects Brent to average about $84 in 2027 as inventories rebuild, but that path remains unusually sensitive to Middle Eastern logistics.

## Hedge funds are becoming a larger node in market plumbing

An IMF financial-stability chapter released October 6 says hedge-fund gross assets have tripled since 2013 to roughly $13 trillion, while gross notional exposures are about $40 trillion. The sector held around 9% of US Treasuries in 2025.

The IMF's concern is not that hedge funds are inherently destabilising. Their flexibility can improve liquidity, price discovery and risk sharing. The vulnerability appears when leveraged and crowded positions meet tighter repo funding, prime-broker credit or derivatives margin requirements.

The feedback loop is familiar: falling prices raise collateral demands; deleveraging forces asset sales; thinner liquidity then amplifies the original price move. The policy question is therefore increasingly about visibility across funds, dealers and markets rather than simply the size of individual funds.

## AI's power constraint moves into long-term contracting

Google and Constellation Energy agreed to bring 890 MW of additional nuclear capacity onto the PJM grid. Constellation plans more than $4.3 billion of upgrades across 11 existing reactor units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey. The package includes a 20-year power purchase agreement and a separate 15-year agreement covering 2,700 MW of energy supply.

This is primarily an uprate strategy rather than construction of a new reactor fleet: equipment and efficiency improvements extract more output from existing nuclear assets. At continuous full output, 890 MW corresponds to roughly 7.8 TWh a year, though actual generation depends on capacity factors, outages and dispatch.

The significance for AI is structural. Compute expansion is no longer constrained only by accelerator availability. Grid connections, transformers, cooling and firm generation are becoming part of the same capital plan, and long-duration power contracts are tying data-centre growth directly to generation-asset economics.

## What matters next

Risk appetite has improved, but four structural pressures remain: expensive duration, depleted oil inventories, growing non-bank leverage and an AI investment cycle that is increasingly committing capital to long-lived energy infrastructure. Earnings, Treasury demand, refined-fuel flows and further power contracts are the next useful tests.
